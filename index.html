JOL: know the rules cold

Who is a Junior Operator?

Any motor vehicle operator or motorcyclist from age 16½ until 18 who holds a Junior Operator's License (JOL). The restriction code is I.

Requirements to obtain a JOL

  • Hold a valid learner's permit for at least six consecutive months before the road test. Any suspension invalidates the permit and restarts the six-month clock after the suspension ends.
  • Maintain a clean driving record for six consecutive months before the road test.
  • Complete RMV-approved driver education: 30 classroom hours + 12 behind-the-wheel hours + 6 observation hours.
  • Complete 40 additional supervised driving hours, certified by a parent/guardian. This drops to 30 hours if an approved driver skills development program was completed.
  • A parent/guardian completes two hours of driver-ed curriculum instruction, unless completed in the past five years.
  • Pass the driver-education final exam so the certificate is filed electronically with the RMV.

JOL license restrictions

  • Passenger restriction: for the first six months after receiving a JOL, no passenger under 18 unless the passenger is immediate family or the driver is accompanied by a person at least 21, licensed in MA or another state, with at least one year of driving experience, seated beside the driver. It ends after six months or at age 18, whichever comes first. A suspension pauses the six-month clock.
  • Night restriction: no driving from 12:30 a.m. to 5:00 a.m. unless accompanied by a parent or legal guardian. From 12:30–1:00 and 4:00–5:00, police generally enforce it only after a lawful stop for another violation, but the driving is still illegal.
  • No vehicle requiring a commercial driver's license.
  • No mobile electronic device for any reason while driving, except reporting an emergency.
  • A suspension imposed while on a JOL must be completed, and the driver must reach 18, before becoming eligible for a full license.
Core ladder: passenger/night restriction violations are 60 days → 180 days → 1 year. JOL speeding is 90 days → 1 year. Drag racing is 1 year → 3 years.

Official manual pp. 9–11.

Alcohol, drugs, BAC, and OUI

What alcohol does

Alcohol is a depressant. It slows reflexes, increases reaction time, distorts vision and judgment, and can create false confidence. It also makes speeding, lane drift, and failure to buckle up more likely. Even one drink in an hour can impair driving; fatigue, emotional upset, and lack of food can worsen the effect.

  • No trick “sobers you up.” Coffee, cold showers, exercise, and food may change how alert you feel, but only time removes alcohol.
  • BAC depends on body weight, amount consumed, food eaten beforehand, time spent drinking, and individual processing speed—not the beverage type.
  • Each contains about the same alcohol (about ½ ounce): 12 oz beer = 5 oz wine = 1½ oz of 80-proof liquor. One may raise an average person's BAC about 0.02.
  • The manual says alcohol makes crash risk about 5× greater; alcohol plus marijuana about 12×.

BAC and implied consent

0.08BAC: criminal legal limit / failed chemical test for all drivers
0.02BAC: under-21 administrative “zero tolerance” threshold
12 · 5 · 1½ounces: beer · wine · 80-proof liquor
Time onlyremoves alcohol from the body

Massachusetts' Implied Consent Law means a licensed driver agrees to chemical testing in covered circumstances. If an officer believes you are OUI, they may request a field sobriety test and, after arrest, a breath or blood test. A BAC above the legal limit—or refusal—causes police to take the license and issue an immediate suspension notice.

Under-21 alcohol, open containers, false IDs, and drugs

  • Under 21: illegal to buy/have someone buy alcohol, or possess/carry/transport alcohol unless with a parent/guardian. License suspension range: 90 days to 1 year. Buying or attempting to buy: 180-day suspension.
  • Open container: neither the driver nor anyone else may have an open alcoholic drink inside the vehicle. Conviction: $100–$500; under 21 also arrested, fined, and suspended.
  • False/altered/borrowed ID or false information: RMV may suspend up to 6 months without a conviction; conviction brings a 1-year suspension.
  • OUI law applies to marijuana, illegal drugs, prescription drugs, and over-the-counter medicines. Legal possession of marijuana does not make impaired driving legal.
  • Marijuana impairs response to sights/sounds, rapid task handling, unexpected-event response, and night vision. Sedatives/cold medicines may cause drowsiness; stimulants may be followed by fatigue, dizziness, nervousness, and poor concentration.

Criminal offense suspension ranges

  • Driving with suspended/revoked license: 60 days–1 year.
  • Operating without owner's authority / vehicle larceny: 1–3 years.
  • Leaving an injury crash: 1–2 years; leaving a property-damage crash: 60 days–1 year.
  • Operating to endanger: 60 days–1 year.
  • Motor vehicle homicide or vehicular manslaughter: 15 years–lifetime.
  • Drug trafficking (except Class D substance): 1–5 years, even without driving.
  • Graffiti/defacing property: 1 year or one-year delay in obtaining a license.
  • Drag racing by drivers over 18: 30–180 days.
Speeding fine formula: minimum fine is $100 plus $5 public-safety surcharge. Over 10 mph above the limit, add $10 for each mph above the first ten. Posted work-zone speeding fines are doubled. The manual also notes a $50 head-injury trust surcharge included in speeding fines.

For a civil motor vehicle infraction, pay or request a hearing within 20 days. A hearing request has a $25 filing fee, refunded if found not responsible for every violation. Ignoring the citation adds a late fee and can lead to suspension.

Official manual pp. 56–68.

Sign shapes you should recognize at a distance

Octagon: stop · downward triangle: yield · pennant: no-passing zone · pentagon: school zone/crossing · diamond: warning · circle: railroad warning · rectangle: regulation or guide/information · crossbuck: railroad crossing · shield: interstate highway.

Traffic lights

Steady red: stop. Right on red after full stop and yielding unless prohibited. Left on red only one-way to one-way, after stop/yield.
Flashing red: treat exactly as a STOP sign.
Steady yellow: stop if safe. If already stopped, do not enter.
Flashing yellow: warning; proceed cautiously. Flashing yellow arrow permits the indicated turn after yielding to traffic and pedestrians.
Steady green: go only if clear; yield to traffic/pedestrians already present. Left turn yields to oncoming traffic.
Green arrow: protected turn, but still check the path.

Blackout: act as if every direction has a stop sign. Pedestrian hybrid beacon: dark = caution; flashing yellow = slow; solid yellow = prepare to stop; solid red pair = stop; flashing red pair = stop, then proceed if clear.

Pavement markings

  • White separates same-direction traffic. Broken white: lane change allowed when safe. Single solid white: should not cross except to avoid danger. Double solid white: crossing prohibited.
  • Yellow separates opposite-direction traffic. Broken yellow: may cross to pass when safe. One solid/one broken: only the side next to the broken line may pass. Double solid yellow: no passing, but may cross to turn left when safe.
  • White diamond: restricted/special lane such as HOV or bus. Sharrows show a shared lane, not a dedicated bike lane.
  • Bicycle lanes usually have solid white lines; cars may enter only when turning on/off the road and must first check for bicyclists. Green pavement highlights bicycle conflict zones; red pavement marks transit use.
  • Stop behind stop lines, yield lines (rows of triangles), and crosswalk lines when required.

At a railroad gate/flashing light, stop at least 15 feet before the post or gate and remain until the gate rises and lights stop. Never go around a lowered gate.

The fundamental speed law always wins: drive no faster than is safe for traffic, road, weather, visibility, pedestrians, and bicyclists—even below a posted limit.

Right-of-way

  • Right-of-way is something you give, not take. Yield to avoid a crash even when legally entitled to go.
  • Uncontrolled intersection: yield to a vehicle already entering or coming from your right.
  • Four-way stop: first full stop goes first; ties yield to the driver on the right.
  • Left turn: yield to oncoming vehicles, vehicles already in the intersection, and pedestrians/bicyclists crossing your path.
  • Entering a paved through road from a private road, driveway, or unpaved road: stop, then yield to everyone on the road/sidewalk.
  • Always yield to pedestrians in the roadway. Fully stop for a blind pedestrian with a white cane or guide dog; do not honk, wave, or pass a stopped vehicle.
  • Rotary/roundabout: counterclockwise traffic; yield to vehicles already inside and to pedestrians. Signal right to exit. Never stop inside; go around again if you miss the exit.

Turns, lanes, passing, and highways

  • Signal a turn at least 100 feet ahead; on a highway, signal at least 500 feet before a turn/exit. Sequence: look (mirrors + blind spot), signal, move.
  • Use the right lane except when passing, turning left, or when it is blocked. Never change lanes in an intersection.
  • Right on red is permitted after full stop and yield unless signed otherwise. Left on red is only from a one-way street to another one-way street.
  • No U-turn near a hill crest/curve or where drivers cannot see 500 feet.
  • Pass left. A two-way broken-yellow pass requires at least 400 feet of clear view, and return right before an oncoming vehicle is within 200 feet. Never speed to pass.
  • Pass right only if the vehicle is turning left, on a one-way street, or on a same-direction multilane road. Never use a shoulder, breakdown lane, or sidewalk to pass.
  • Highway entry: yield to traffic already on the highway and match its speed. Missed exit: continue to the next exit; never stop or back up.

School bus, following distance, and parking

  • Flashing red school-bus lights/extended stop sign: both directions stop until signals end. Exception: opposite side of a divided highway with a physical barrier. First violation can mean a $250 fine and suspension.
  • Minimum following gap in good conditions: 3 seconds; motorcycles: at least 4 seconds; heavy equipment: at least 10 seconds. Increase for poor conditions.
  • Park within 12 inches of the curb and leave at least 12 feet of clear road.
  • No parking within 20 feet of an intersection or 10 feet of a hydrant/fire lane, or in a crosswalk, driveway, bike/bus/taxi/loading zone, sidewalk/median, wrong direction, or double-parked.
  • Hill wheels: no curb = inward toward road edge; uphill with curb = outward toward travel lane; downhill with curb = inward toward curb.

Official manual pp. 79–110.

Special situations and safety traps

Emergency vehicles and move-over law
  • Moving emergency vehicle with siren/lights: pull as far right as possible and stop. If in an intersection, clear it first, then pull over. Do not follow closer than 300 feet.
  • Stationary emergency/maintenance/recovery vehicle with flashing lights: slow to a reasonable safe speed. On a highway with at least four lanes (two in your direction), move over one lane when safe; otherwise slow and use caution.
  • Do not drive by or park within 800 feet of a fire. Never cross an unprotected fire hose unless directed.
Headlights, weather, and visibility
  • Use headlights/taillights from ½ hour after sunset to ½ hour before sunrise, whenever visibility is under 500 feet, whenever wipers are used, in rain/snow/fog, and in tunnels.
  • High beams show about 350 feet; low beams about 100 feet. Dim high beams within 500 feet of oncoming traffic or 200 feet behind a vehicle.
  • Fog: use low beams. Hydroplaning: ease off gas; do not brake or turn suddenly. Bridges/overpasses freeze first.
Skids and mechanical emergencies
  • Skid: ease off gas, shift to neutral, do not brake, steer in the direction of the skid.
  • Run off pavement: hold wheel, ease off gas, brake gently, check traffic, return gently.
  • Blowout: grip wheel, ease off gas, gently straighten; avoid braking until controlled, then pull off.
  • Brake failure: downshift, gently use parking brake while holding release, warn others. Pump only non-ABS brakes—never pump ABS.
  • Stuck accelerator: shift to neutral, brake, try to free pedal, then pull over.
Crashes and reports
  • Always stop. For property-only crashes, move vehicles from travel lanes if possible and exchange identity, license, registration, and insurance details.
  • Within 5 days, report to both RMV and local police if there is injury/death or at least $1,000 property damage.
  • Do not move an injured person unless necessary for immediate safety; support head/spine if movement is unavoidable.
Seat belts, child restraints, and phones
  • Everyone in a passenger vehicle must buckle up or use an appropriate child restraint. Driver and unbelted passengers age 16+ may each be fined $25; driver may be fined $25 for each unbelted passenger age 12–15.
  • Child restraint until at least age 8 or 57 inches tall; then safety belt. Rear-facing infant seat never in front of an active passenger air bag.
  • Drivers 18+ may use only hands-free mounted devices, with a single touch to activate; handheld use is not allowed even at red lights/stop signs. Under 18: no device use except emergency reporting.
  • General handheld penalties: $100 / $250 / $500, with education from the second offense and insurance surcharge from the third.

Official manual pp. 35–47 and 119–131.

Self-test: answer before revealing

1. What are the five official exam subject areas?

Alcohol misuse, suspensions, JOL violations, rules of the road, and identifying road signs.

2. A JOL driver carries a 17-year-old friend two months after getting licensed. Who must be present for this to be legal?

A person at least 21, licensed in MA or another state, with at least one year of driving experience, seated beside the driver. (Immediate family is exempt.)

3. What is the JOL passenger/night restriction suspension ladder?

60 days first, 180 days second, one year subsequent.

4. What is the JOL speeding suspension ladder?

90 days first, one year second or subsequent.

5. What are the adult and under-21 BAC thresholds?

0.08 for a failed chemical test / criminal limit for all drivers; 0.02 for under-21 administrative zero-tolerance sanctions.

6. Which removes alcohol faster: coffee, food, exercise, or a cold shower?

None. Only time removes alcohol.

7. With no prior OUI, how long is a chemical-test refusal suspension for someone over 21? For someone 18–21?

Over 21: 180 days. Age 18–21: three years plus 180 days.

8. What does a flashing red light mean? A flashing yellow?

Flashing red: full stop, then proceed under right-of-way rules. Flashing yellow: warning—proceed cautiously.

9. What do white and yellow pavement lines separate?

White: traffic in the same direction. Yellow: traffic in opposite directions.

10. Two cars stop simultaneously at a four-way stop. Who goes first?

The vehicle on the right.

11. When must both directions stop for a school bus, and what is the exception?

Stop while red lights flash / stop sign is extended. Exception: the bus is across a divided highway with a physical barrier.

12. What are the default school, settled-district, outside-district, and highway speed limits?

20, 30, 40, and 50 mph, unless posted otherwise (some settled communities post 25).

13. When may you turn left on red?

Only from a one-way street onto another one-way street, after a full stop and yielding, unless prohibited.

14. How far ahead do you signal a normal turn? A highway exit?

At least 100 feet normally; 500 feet on a highway.

15. At what distances must you dim high beams?

Within 500 feet of oncoming traffic or within 200 feet behind another vehicle.

16. What should you do in a skid?

Ease off gas, shift to neutral, do not brake, and steer in the direction of the skid.

17. Where do you pull for a moving emergency vehicle? What if you're in an intersection?

Pull as far right as possible and stop. If in an intersection, clear it first, then pull right and stop.

18. What crash must be reported, to whom, and by when?

Any crash with injury/death or at least $1,000 damage; report to both RMV and local police within five days.

19. Until what age/height must a child use a child restraint?

Until at least age eight or 57 inches tall.

20. What are the first three handheld-device fine amounts?

$100 first, $250 second, $500 third/subsequent. JOL suspensions and reinstatement requirements also apply to under-18 drivers.
